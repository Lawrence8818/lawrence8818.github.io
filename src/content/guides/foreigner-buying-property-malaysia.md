I get this question every week, mostly from Singaporeans, Hong Kong and mainland Chinese buyers, and from Malaysians asking on behalf of a foreign spouse. The short answer is yes, a foreigner can buy and own property in Malaysia in their own name. The longer answer is that each state sets its own conditions, and the taxes are heavier than for locals.

Everything below is what I checked in November 2026. State rules in particular are changed by circular without much publicity, so use this as a map and have your lawyer confirm before you commit money.

## The minimum price depends on the state

Land is a state matter in Malaysia, so there is no single national figure. These are the commonly published thresholds for the areas I am asked about most:

| State | Minimum price for foreigners |
| --- | --- |
| Kuala Lumpur | RM1 million |
| Selangor | RM2 million in most Klang Valley districts (Petaling, Gombak, Hulu Langat, Sepang, Klang and others); RM1 million in the outer districts. Generally strata and landed-strata homes only |
| Johor | Generally RM1 million, with different figures for some zones and for landed property |
| Penang | Island: RM1 million for strata, RM3 million for landed. Mainland: RM500,000 for strata, RM1 million for landed |

A few honest caveats. Selangor has adjusted its strata threshold before, and published guides do not all agree on the current figure. Johor has special zones with their own rules. So please do not rely on a table, including mine, for a purchase that sits close to the line.

The threshold is checked against the purchase price. If a developer rebate brings your net price close to the minimum, ask your lawyer how the state treats it before you sign.

## State consent

Every foreign purchase needs approval from the state authority before the property can be transferred to you. Your lawyer applies after the SPA is signed.

- It often takes a few months, and timing varies by state.
- Some states charge a consent fee or levy. Johor and Penang, for example, charge foreign buyers a percentage of the price. Ask for the current rate.
- If consent is refused, the SPA should say you get your money back. Check that clause before signing.

## What foreigners cannot buy

- Property on Malay Reserve land
- Units allocated to the Bumiputera quota (Bumi lots)
- Low-cost and low-medium-cost housing, and most affordable-housing schemes
- Anything priced below the state minimum
- In some states, certain landed types or agricultural land

Developers may also have a cap on how many units in a project can go to foreign buyers.

## Financing

Foreigners can borrow from Malaysian banks, but expect:

- A lower margin, commonly around 60% to 70%. It varies by bank and by your profile.
- More documents: passport, income proof, bank statements, sometimes a credit report from your home country.
- A tenure limited by your age, the same as for locals.

Many foreign buyers pay cash or borrow in their home country instead. Either way, budget your own funds at 30% to 40% of the price plus the costs below.

## Taxes that are different for foreigners

**Stamp duty on the transfer (MOT).** Malaysians pay a tiered 1% to 4%. For non-citizens (permanent residents excluded), the rate on residential property was raised from a flat 4% to a flat 8% from 1 January 2026. What matters is the date of the transfer document, not only the SPA date.

A quick comparison on a RM1.2 million condo:

| Buyer | MOT stamp duty |
| --- | --- |
| Malaysian | RM32,000 |
| Foreigner | RM96,000 (8%) |

**Real Property Gains Tax (RPGT) when you sell.**

| Sold in | Citizens and PRs | Foreigners |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Years 1 to 3 | 30% | 30% |
| Year 4 | 20% | 30% |
| Year 5 | 15% | 30% |
| Year 6 onwards | 0% | 10% |

The tax is on the gain, not the selling price. But for a foreigner it never reaches zero, so Malaysian property works better as a long hold than a quick flip.

Loan agreement stamp duty (0.5% of the loan) and legal fees are the same as for locals.

## MM2H in brief

Malaysia My Second Home is a long-stay visa. It is separate from the right to buy property.

Under the current structure there are several tiers (Silver, Gold, Platinum, plus a special zone category), each with a fixed deposit requirement and a minimum property purchase that must be held for a number of years. The published minimums are RM600,000, RM1 million and RM2 million for the three main tiers.

The MM2H property minimum and the state's foreign-buyer minimum are two separate rules, and some states treat MM2H holders differently. If the visa is your goal, speak to a licensed MM2H agent before choosing a property, not after.

## A sensible order to do things

1. Decide the state and area, then confirm the current threshold and allowed property types.
2. Check your financing, or confirm your cash position, before paying a booking fee.
3. Work out the full cost with 8% stamp duty included.
4. Use a lawyer who handles foreign purchases regularly, and make sure the SPA covers a refund if consent is refused.
