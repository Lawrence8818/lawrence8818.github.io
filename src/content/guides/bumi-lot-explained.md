"Why is that unit cheaper than this one, same layout, same floor?" When a buyer asks me that at a sales gallery, the answer is very often that one of them is a Bumi lot.

It is one of the most misunderstood parts of buying property in Malaysia, by Bumiputera and non-Bumiputera buyers alike. Here is how it works in plain terms.

## What a Bumi lot is

When a developer gets approval for a housing project, the state usually requires a share of the units to be reserved for Bumiputera buyers and sold to them at a discount. Those reserved units are the Bumi lots.

Bumiputera generally means Malays, the natives of Sabah and Sarawak, and Orang Asli. The exact definition used for housing purposes comes from the state, and some states have their own conditions for mixed-heritage buyers. If you are unsure whether you qualify, ask before you book.

## The quota and the discount

Both are set by each state, not by the federal government, and can also vary with the type of development.

- **Quota**: 30% of the units is the figure you will hear most, but it is higher in some states and for some project types.
- **Discount**: commonly quoted residential figures are 7% in Selangor, 15% in Johor and 5% in Penang. For Kuala Lumpur, published sources quote between 5% and 7%.

Treat these as a guide. The figure that counts is the one in the developer's approval for that specific project.

A simple example: on a unit listed at RM500,000 in a state with a 7% discount, a Bumiputera buyer pays RM465,000. That is RM35,000 saved on the price, and a lower loan amount, down payment and instalment to match.

## The trade-off: resale

The discount is not free money. A Bumi lot normally keeps its status after the first sale.

- You can generally sell only to another Bumiputera buyer.
- To sell to a non-Bumi, the owner must apply for state consent. Approval is not automatic, the conditions differ by state, and it can take months.
- A smaller pool of buyers can mean a longer time to sell and less room to negotiate on price.

So if you are a Bumiputera buyer, ask yourself whether this is a long-term home or something you expect to sell in a few years. For own stay, the discount is a real benefit. For a short hold, weigh the discount against the narrower exit.

## What "Bumi release" means

If the developer cannot sell the Bumi units after a period of genuine effort, it can apply to the state to release them from the quota so they can be sold on the open market.

How this works varies a lot by state. In general:

- The developer has to show that the units were marketed to Bumiputera buyers and that the project has reached a certain construction stage.
- The state may release only some of the units, or release them in batches.
- The developer usually pays a levy or contribution to the state for each unit released.
- Once released, the unit is sold without the Bumi discount.

In Selangor, for example, the application goes through the state housing board. Other states use the land office or the state housing department.

## What non-Bumi buyers should know

1. **You cannot book a Bumi lot and "sort it out later".** If a unit is still under the quota, the sale to you cannot be completed. Do not pay a booking fee on a verbal promise that the release is coming.
2. **Ask whether the unit is a released lot.** If it is, ask to see the state's release approval. A properly released unit is an ordinary unit, and you can resell it to anyone.
3. **Released units are often the leftovers.** They come to the market late in construction, so the choice of floor and facing may be limited. On the other hand, you see more of the building before you commit and wait less for the keys.
4. **In a subsale, check before the deposit.** If the seller is Bumiputera and the unit was bought with a Bumi discount, the transfer to you may need state consent. Your lawyer should confirm this before you sign anything.

## How to check a unit's status

- **New project**: ask the developer for the approved list or layout showing which units are Bumi quota. It should also be reflected in the SPA.
- **Subsale**: have your lawyer do a title search and check the restrictions on the title. The Bumi status is not always printed there, especially for strata units, so the lawyer may also need to confirm with the developer, the land office or the state housing board.
- **Still unsure**: check the original SPA. A Bumi discount on the purchase price is a clear sign.

## Do not confuse it with Malay Reserve land

A Bumi lot is a quota condition on a unit. Malay Reserve land is a status of the land itself, and it can only be owned by Malays as defined by that state's law. Reserve land is not something a developer can apply to release in the same way. If a listing is unusually cheap, check which of the two you are looking at.
