When buyers ask me "how much cash do I need besides the down payment", the two biggest answers are almost always stamp duty and legal fees. Neither is a mystery. Both follow published scales, so you can work out your own figure before you sign anything.

The numbers below are what I checked in November 2026. Tax rules change with each Budget, so treat this as a guide and get the final figure from your lawyer.

## The four items you are paying for

1. **MOT stamp duty**: tax on the transfer of ownership into your name.
2. **Loan agreement stamp duty**: tax on your loan document.
3. **SPA legal fee**: your lawyer's fee for the Sale and Purchase Agreement and the transfer.
4. **Loan agreement legal fee**: the fee for the bank's loan documentation.

The SPA itself only carries a small fixed stamping fee per copy, so it is not the one to worry about.

## MOT stamp duty

It is tiered, like income tax. Each band is charged at its own rate:

| Portion of the price | Rate |
| --- | --- |
| First RM100,000 | 1% |
| RM100,001 to RM500,000 | 2% |
| RM500,001 to RM1,000,000 | 3% |
| Above RM1,000,000 | 4% |

This scale applies to Malaysian citizens and permanent residents. For foreign buyers, a flat 8% applies to residential property from 1 January 2026.

For a new launch, the MOT is usually stamped later, when the title is ready to be transferred to you, not on the day you sign the SPA.

## Loan agreement stamp duty

A flat 0.5% of the loan amount. No tiers.

## Legal fees

Legal fees follow the Solicitors' Remuneration Order 2023, in force since 15 July 2023. The same scale is used twice, once on the purchase price (SPA) and once on the loan amount (loan agreement):

- First RM500,000: 1.25% (minimum RM500)
- Next RM7,000,000: 1%
- Above RM7.5 million: negotiable

On top of the fee you pay service tax on the legal fee (8% at the time of writing) and disbursements such as searches, registration and travel, which are usually from a few hundred ringgit to over a thousand per file.

Two things to know. The Order allows a limited discount, so quotations do differ between firms. And purchases from a developer under the Housing Development Act fall under a reduced scale, which is one reason developers can afford to absorb the fee in a package. Ask your lawyer for the exact figure for your case.

## A worked example

A RM600,000 property with a 90% loan of RM540,000, bought by a Malaysian who is not a first-time buyer:

| Item | Calculation | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MOT stamp duty | (100,000 × 1%) + (400,000 × 2%) + (100,000 × 3%) | RM12,000 |
| Loan agreement stamp duty | 540,000 × 0.5% | RM2,700 |
| SPA legal fee | (500,000 × 1.25%) + (100,000 × 1%) | RM7,250 |
| Loan agreement legal fee | (500,000 × 1.25%) + (40,000 × 1%) | RM6,650 |
| **Total before service tax and disbursements** | | **RM28,600** |

Add roughly RM1,100 of service tax on the two legal fees, plus disbursements, and you are near RM30,000, about 5% of the price. That is full scale with no discount and no developer package.

## What first-time buyers get

As of November 2026:

- **Homes up to RM500,000**: 100% exemption on both the MOT and the loan agreement stamp duty, for Malaysian citizens buying their first residential property, where the SPA is signed on or before 31 December 2027.
- **Above RM500,000**: no exemption under the current rule. The exemption does not apply to "the first RM500,000" of a more expensive home.

Budget 2027, tabled on 9 October 2026, proposed two changes for SPAs signed from 1 January 2027 to 31 December 2030: keeping the full exemption up to RM500,000, and for homes above RM500,000 up to RM750,000, a full exemption on the first RM500,000 plus 50% off the remainder. On the RM600,000 example, the MOT would drop from RM12,000 to about RM1,500.

That is still a proposal as I write this. Until the exemption order is gazetted, the details can change, so do not sign on the assumption that it applies to you.

The exemption only covers stamp duty. Legal fees are still payable unless the developer absorbs them.

## How to budget

- Work out the full-scale figure first, then subtract what the developer's package covers in writing.
- Ask whether "free legal fees" includes the loan agreement, and who pays the stamp duty and disbursements.
- If you are a first-time buyer near RM500,000, check the SPA price carefully. A price slightly above the cap loses the whole exemption under the current rule.
- From 1 January 2027, stamping of property transfers moves to a self-assessment system. Your lawyer handles it, and the rates do not change, but ask them to confirm timing.
