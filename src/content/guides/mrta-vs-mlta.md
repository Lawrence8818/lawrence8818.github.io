Almost every buyer I work with gets the same question from the banker at loan stage: "MRTA, how much do you want to cover?" Most people say yes without really knowing what they bought, or that there is an alternative.

Both products do the same basic job. If you pass away or suffer total permanent disability (TPD) during the loan, the insurance pays so your family is not left with the debt. The way they do it is quite different.

## What each one is

**MRTA (Mortgage Reducing Term Assurance)** is cover that shrinks over time, roughly following your outstanding loan. It is usually sold by the bank, paid as one lump-sum premium at the start, and the bank is the beneficiary.

**MLTA (Mortgage Level Term Assurance)** is cover that stays the same for the whole term. It is usually bought from an insurance agent, paid by regular premiums, and the policy belongs to you.

The Islamic equivalents are MRTT and MLTT. The structure is takaful, but the comparison below is broadly the same.

## The differences side by side

| | MRTA | MLTA |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coverage shape | Reduces every year | Stays level |
| Who gets paid | The bank, up to the outstanding loan; any balance goes to your estate | The bank is paid what is owed; the rest goes to the people you nominate |
| Premium | Usually one single premium upfront | Yearly or monthly |
| Financed into the loan | Commonly yes | Normally no |
| Transferable | Tied to that one loan | Can follow you to the next property |
| Cash value | Generally none | Some plans have cash value or a refund feature |
| Cost | Lower | Noticeably higher for the same starting cover |

Policy features vary by insurer, so read the product disclosure sheet for the plan you are actually offered.

## A simple example of "coverage shape"

Take a RM450,000 loan over 35 years at an illustrative 4% a year.

After 10 years of normal repayment, the outstanding loan is roughly RM377,000.

- With **MRTA** that was set up to match the loan, the cover at year 10 is about that same amount. A claim clears the loan and there is little or nothing left over.
- With **MLTA** of RM450,000, the cover is still RM450,000. A claim clears the RM377,000 and about RM73,000 goes to your family. The gap gets bigger every year.

By year 25, the loan is down to roughly RM197,000. MRTA still clears it. MLTA clears it and leaves more than RM250,000 for your family.

## Three things about MRTA people miss

1. **The reducing schedule is fixed on day one.** It is calculated on an assumed interest rate. If your actual loan rate rises, or you fall behind on payments, the outstanding loan can end up higher than the cover, and your family pays the difference.
2. **You choose how much and how long.** You can cover the full loan for the full tenure, or only part of it. A cheaper quote often means partial cover. Ask "what amount, for how many years, at what assumed rate".
3. **Financing it is not free.** Adding the premium to the loan means paying interest on it for decades.

## Who each one suits

MRTA usually fits if:

- you want the lowest cost and your only goal is clearing the loan
- it is an own-stay home you plan to keep for a long time
- you already have enough life insurance for your family elsewhere

MLTA usually fits if:

- you expect to sell, upgrade or refinance within 5 to 10 years
- you are buying more than one property over time and want cover that moves with you
- you are the main income earner and want your family to receive something beyond a cleared loan
- you can manage the regular premium comfortably

Some buyers do a mix: a smaller MRTA to satisfy the bank's package, plus their own life policy for the family.

## Questions to ask before you sign

- Is the loan rate different with and without MRTA?
- What sum and how many years does this quote cover?
- What interest rate is the reducing schedule based on?
- For a joint loan, how is the cover split between us?
- If I settle the loan early, what refund do I get?
- Does it cover TPD and critical illness, or death only?

Also be honest in the health declaration. A claim can be rejected for non-disclosure, and that defeats the whole purpose.
