## Key takeaways

- Fully or partly furnished depends first on whether you will live in the unit or rent it out.
- Investing: if the price gap is small, take fully furnished so you can rent it out sooner.
- Own stay: with some cash set aside for renovation, partly furnished is enough; with plenty of funds, a bare unit is a good option too.
- Own-stay buyers who take fully furnished often end up removing furniture they dislike and replacing the air-conditioners after getting the keys.

## What I say in the video

Someone asked me today: should I choose fully furnished or partly furnished?

Many projects now are mostly partly furnished, which gives you roughly 60 to 70 per cent of the furnishing, or else fully furnished. How do you choose? It depends a great deal on whether you will live there yourself or invest.

Some clients have shared this with me. A few years ago they bought a project without thinking it through and took the fully furnished package. The unit turned out to be for their own stay, and they ended up taking out a lot of the furniture because they did not like the colour or the design.

So if you are buying to invest, and the price difference between fully and partly furnished is not large, I suggest fully furnished. When you get the unit you can rent it out faster, because there is not much left for you to do.

If it is for your own stay and you have some cash ready for renovation, I suggest partly furnished. If your funds are very comfortable, a bare unit is also a very good option.

So if you ask me: for own stay, partly furnished is enough, and you do not need fully furnished. Otherwise you end up like that client of mine. After getting the keys he removed things because he did not like the colour, he did not like some of the fittings in the kitchen, and he wanted to change the air-conditioners to higher-horsepower ones.

Go back to your own needs, and decide based on whether it is for own stay or for investment.
