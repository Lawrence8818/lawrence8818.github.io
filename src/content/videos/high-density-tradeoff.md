## Key takeaways

- High density usually means a project of 2,000 to 3,000 units; under 500 units is low density.
- The upside: more facilities, a maintenance fee that is usually lower, and a friendlier price.
- One cost: with so many residents, the daily wait for the lift is long.
- Another cost: when you sell, many owners in the same project are selling too, which can turn into a price war.

## What I say in the video

One client chose a high-density project because it had plenty of facilities and a friendly price. A year after moving in, he told me the wait for the lift was too long, and the part he could not stand was waiting for the lift every morning.

Many condominiums now have two towers and about 1,000 units. Could the developer build just one tower? Yes. But if a unit sells for RM 500,000 today, turning two towers into one means it has to be sold to you at a million. Why? Because all the costs are already there.

First, what counts as high density. High density is usually 2,000 to 3,000 units. Below 500 units is low density. The more units and the more residents, the bigger the difference in how it feels to live there.

With high density, the maintenance fee is usually lower and the price is friendlier. The other side is the lift: you will wait a long time, because so many people live there.

There is one more thing few people think about. When you want to sell and there are two or three thousand units, you will not be the only seller, and that brings the problem of a price war.

Get this clear before you buy, so that you are not worn out while living there and stuck when you sell.
