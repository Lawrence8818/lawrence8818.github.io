## Key takeaways

- When viewing, ask not only whether you will be comfortable, but how someone else will see the unit when you sell or rent it out.
- What surrounds the property, such as a high-voltage pylon or a sewage tank, directly affects resale price and how fast it rents.
- A unit that will not rent is often not a renovation problem but a surroundings problem.
- Take one more look around for your future self, so that you are not stuck when it is time to exit.

## What I say in the video

I asked a buyer: if you had to sell this property today and the next buyer arrived downstairs and saw a high-voltage pylon, would he use that to push your price down? He thought for a few seconds and said: “Why didn’t I think of that back then?”

Many people view a home thinking only about whether they will be comfortable living there. But you may one day sell it or rent it out, and then it is another pair of eyes looking at your property.

I had an owner who wanted to rent out his unit. He told me that after two months he still had no tenant, while the block next door rented out quickly. So I took him downstairs to look. Right in front was a sewage tank, and his unit faced it. Tenants walked in, turned around and left.

Very often it is not about how nice your renovation is. It is about what is around your property.

So when you view a home, besides asking whether you like it, take one more look on behalf of your future self: what is around you? The extra few steps you take today are so that when you need to exit, this home does not trap you.
