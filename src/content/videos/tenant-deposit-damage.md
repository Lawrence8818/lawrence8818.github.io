## Key takeaways

- When a tenant damages the unit and the owner cannot deduct from the deposit, it is usually because the tenancy agreement did not spell it out.
- Malaysia has no dedicated tenancy act for now, so what is written in the agreement is your only basis.
- Before renting out, state clearly: when the deposit can be deducted or refunded, what happens on early termination, the move-out inspection and the standard for restoring the unit, and who pays for which repairs.
- A clearly written agreement is the best protection for a landlord’s cash flow.

## What I say in the video

Your tenant damages your home, you want to deduct the cost from the deposit, and you find that you cannot.

I met an owner whose tenant left the walls marked and the furniture broken. He wanted to deduct from the deposit, which sounds reasonable. But when he checked the tenancy agreement, there was no clause at all on restoring the unit to its original condition. The tenant refused to accept it, there was nothing he could do, and he had to bear the repair cost himself.

Malaysia still has no specific tenancy act. Whatever is written in your tenancy agreement is the only law you have.

Before you rent out, a few points must be written clearly: when the deposit can be deducted and when it is refunded; what happens if the tenant ends the tenancy early; most importantly, the inspection before move-out and the standard for restoring the unit; and repair responsibility, meaning what the landlord pays for and what the tenant pays for. All of this should be in the agreement from the start.

It is not difficult at all. The trouble comes later, when something is damaged and you cannot deduct for it, and you end up bearing the cost alone.

You are renting out a property. A clearly written tenancy agreement is the best protection for your cash flow.
