## Key takeaways

- Sell within five years of buying and Real Property Gains Tax (RPGT) applies, based on the gain (sale price minus purchase price) and the year in which you sell.
- Citizens and permanent residents who sell in the sixth year or later are exempt.
- Everyone has a once-in-a-lifetime exemption that can be used on one private residence, whatever the gain.
- If you own more than one property, save that single exemption for the one with the largest gain.

## What I say in the video

You sell your home, receive a sum of money, and assume all of it goes into your pocket? Not quite. If you sell within five years of buying, there is a tax that takes part of it.

The tax is called RPGT, Real Property Gains Tax. The calculation is simple: your sale price minus your purchase price, taxed according to which year you sell in.

If you sell in the sixth year or later, citizens and permanent residents are exempt. So one year can make a real difference in money. Selling in the fifth year and selling in the sixth are not the same.

What if you cannot wait until the sixth year and need to sell in the first few years? Here is some good news: there is a once-in-a-lifetime exemption. You can choose one private residence and, whatever the gain, be fully exempt from this tax. But remember, it is once in a lifetime. Use it and it is gone. If you have more than one property you might sell, keep it for the one with the largest gain and do not waste it on a small amount.

I studied finance, and this is exactly what I watch for: you think the money is in hand, and then another portion is deducted. Think through when to sell, so that you know where you stand and are not thrown off by a tax you did not count.
